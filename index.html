Pulled apart, the smog looks small

Bad-air days are about 13% of all days. That share hides how unevenly they fall. They pile up in a handful of places.

171 cities, one disc each

Each disc is a city's year. Bad days sit at the centre, clear days around them, and unrecorded days form a faint outer ring.

They are sorted from the most bad days to the fewest. Hover any disc to read it.

Nine of the ten worst ring Delhi

Bhiwadi had 213 bad days, more than half its year. Ghaziabad had 202. Greater Noida, Faridabad, Meerut, Baghpat, Bulandshahr, Delhi and Noida each had between 159 and 180.

The odd one out is Singrauli, a coal-power town in Madhya Pradesh, with 153.

Put them on a map

Placed where they are, the cities crowd together across the north. Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Punjab and Chandigarh hold 5,766 of the 8,096 bad days. That is 71%.

The south mostly breathed easy

Bengaluru, Chennai and Hyderabad logged all 365 days at AQI 200 or below. In total, 25 cities never recorded a day above 200 in 2021.

Bihar's year is mostly blank

Seventeen Bihar cities were monitored for fewer than 60 days. Araria has 14 recorded days out of 365.

In 16 of those 17 cities, bad days outnumber clear ones among the days that were measured. A blank day is an unknown day, not a clean one.

Second dataset · indoors

Now step inside

A 2024 study in BMC Geriatrics used the Longitudinal Ageing Study in India: 72,250 adults aged 45 and over, surveyed in 2017–18. It scored each household's indoor smoke from unclean cooking fuel and stoves without ventilation.

The violet haze shows each state's band in that study, from 1 to 4. Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand and Odisha sit in the top band.

Where the two smokes meet

Of the 32 cities with 100 or more bad outdoor days, 31 are in states the study puts in its top two bands for cooking smoke. The 32nd is Delhi, too small to read on its map.

Older adults across Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and Bihar breathe smoke on both sides of the front door.

They are not the same map

Indoor smoke peaks further east. Odisha, Jharkhand and Mizoram are in the top cooking-smoke band, yet their few monitors logged under 4% bad outdoor days. Across 24 states, the two rankings barely agree (Spearman 0.21).

Bihar has both: the top indoor band, and an outdoor record that is mostly blank.

The north sleeps worst

The study's sleep-problem scores are highest in Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and West Bengal, all in its top band (0.84+ on a 0–4 scale). Most of the outdoor smog sits there too.

Karnataka is the outlier. It is in the top sleep band, while its 22 cities logged under 1% bad days.

Clean air, sharper scores

Kerala and Tamil Nadu are the only states in the study's top cognition band (28+ on a 3–43 scale). Their 12 monitored cities logged 8 bad days between them in 2021.

Telangana breaks the pattern. Hyderabad had 365 clear days, but the study places Telangana in its third cooking-smoke band and names it the one southern state with low cognition scores.

What the study measured

Indoor smoke was linked to lower cognition, partly through depression and poor sleep. In its fullest model, 12.8% of the effect ran through those two.

Read the overlay as geography, not cause. The study is cross-sectional, its bands are 2017–18 state averages, and it did not measure outdoor air.

Shaw, Kundu, Chattopadhyay & Rao (2024). Indoor air pollution and cognitive function among older adults in India. BMC Geriatrics 24:81. doi.org/10.1186/s12877-024-04662-6
